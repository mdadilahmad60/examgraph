About this tool

Updated November 2026 · Free, no sign-up

Find the best-fit parabola for your data. Get a, b, c and R², then read the vertex and roots straight from the graph, free online.

Quadratic regression fits a parabola y = ax² + bx + c to data that rises and then falls, or falls and then rises: projectile heights, profit against price, or the area of a rectangle with a fixed perimeter.

The sample data below is a ball's height over time. Fit it, then look under Key points: the vertex (maximum height) and the roots (when it hits the ground) are listed for the fitted curve.

Use quadratic regression when the data clearly bends once, rising then falling or the reverse. Check the scatter plot first: if the points follow a straight line or keep curving the same way, a different model may fit better. Compare R-squared across models, but also ask whether a parabola makes sense for the situation.

  • A parabola needs at least three points; more points give a more trustworthy fit.
  • The sign of the leading coefficient a shows whether the curve opens up or down.
  • The vertex gives the maximum or minimum of the fitted curve, listed under Key points.
  • Be careful about predicting far outside the range of your data: parabolas eventually turn around.

Closely related topics are covered by Linear Regression Calculator, Line of Best Fit & Scatter Plot Maker and Exponential Regression Calculator.

Solved examples

Fit a parabola to height data

Problem. Fit a quadratic to (0, 1), (1, 5.2), (2, 7.4), (3, 7.9), (4, 6.1), (5, 2.5) and find the peak.

  1. Choose Quadratic in the regression panel. The fit is about y = −0.98x² + 5.22x + 0.98, with R² ≈ 0.999.
  2. a is negative, so the parabola opens downward and has a maximum.
  3. Vertex: x = −b/(2a) = 5.216/(2·0.982) ≈ 2.66, and y(2.66) ≈ 7.91.

Answer. The peak is at about x = 2.66 with a height of about 7.9. The very high R² says a parabola describes this data well. Graph it in the calculator

Maximum height and landing time of a projectile

Problem. A model for height in meters is h = −4.9t² + 20t + 1. Find the maximum height and when it lands.

  1. Maximum at t = −b/(2a) = 20/9.8 ≈ 2.04 s.
  2. h(2.04) = −4.9(2.04)² + 20(2.04) + 1 ≈ 21.4 m.
  3. Landing is the positive root of −4.9t² + 20t + 1 = 0: t = (20 + √(400 + 19.6))/9.8 ≈ 4.13 s.

Answer. Maximum height about 21.4 m at 2.04 s, landing at about 4.13 s. Graph it in the calculator

How to use the Quadratic Regression Calculator

  • Type an equation. Write it in terms of x, for example x^2 - 4 or 2x + 1. Circles, ellipses and inequalities work too: try x^2 + y^2 = 9 or y > 2x + 1 (shaded). On a phone, tap the equation box and use the built-in math keyboard for powers, roots, fractions and trig.
  • Compare curves. Tap + to add more equations. Intersections, roots, intercepts and turning points are marked automatically, and you can tap any entry under Key points to jump to it.
  • Use sliders. Any extra letter becomes a slider, so a*x^2 + b*x + c lets you drag a, b and c.
  • Move around. Drag to pan, pinch or scroll to zoom, or use the + / − buttons. The home button resets the view and the corner button opens a full-screen graph.
  • Plot points and fit a regression. Type a point such as (2, 5) or a list like (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 5). For real data, open Data table & regression, paste x, y pairs and pick linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential, power or logarithmic: the best-fit equation, R² and a prediction box appear instantly.
  • Degrees or radians. Tap RAD / DEG beside the equations to switch angle mode.
  • Name, share or save. Edit the graph title above the plot. Settings lets you download a PNG or JPG, or open a print-ready page to save as PDF. The share button copies a link that reopens your equations and graph title.
  • Practice for tests. Turn on US Exam Mode in Settings to lock key-point hints and tooltips, like a real testing calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How many points do I need?

At least 3 for a quadratic. More points give a more reliable fit.

How do I find the maximum of the fitted curve?

The fitted equation is added to the graph, and its max or min appears under Key points. Tap it to jump to that point.

Quadratic or exponential?

If the data changes direction, use quadratic. If it grows or shrinks by a constant percentage, use exponential. Try both and compare R².

How do I know if a quadratic is the right model?

The scatter plot should show a single bend. If R-squared is much higher than for a linear fit and the shape makes sense for the situation, a quadratic is reasonable.
