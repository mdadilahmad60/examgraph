About this tool

Updated November 2026 · Free, no sign-up

Practice North Carolina EOG and EOC math with a free online graphing calculator. Plot equations, check your answers and learn the test rules.

North Carolina students take end-of-grade (EOG) math tests in the middle grades and end-of-course (EOC) tests for high-school math. The EOC tests cover NC Math 1 and NC Math 3 (there is no separate NC Math 2 EOC). NC Math 1 has a calculator-inactive section and a calculator-active section, NC Math 3 is calculator-active throughout, and students must be provided a graphing calculator for both, which can be the online one built into the test. The tests are given by the state, and the calculator rules differ by test, grade and testing format. Because those rules can change from year to year, always check the current policy from the NC Department of Public Instruction or your teacher before relying on any calculator.

This page gives you a free graphing calculator for practice. Use it to see how linear functions, quadratics and exponentials behave, to find where two graphs intersect, and to read the vertex, roots and intercepts of a function. The starter graph pairs a line with a parabola, so you can already practice solving a linear-quadratic system by finding where they cross. Turn on US Exam Mode in Settings to practice without hints, which is closer to a real testing situation.

  • Start with the function families in NC Math 1: linear, quadratic and exponential.
  • Use intersections to solve systems, and Key points to check intercepts and vertices.
  • Practice reading a graph first, then confirm the algebra rather than the other way around.
  • Try US Exam Mode to see whether you can finish without the hints.

You may also find these tools useful: Texas STAAR Algebra I Graphing Calculator, NY Regents Algebra I Graphing Calculator, Georgia Milestones EOC Practice Calculator, Virginia SOL Math Graphing Calculator and Smarter Balanced (SBAC) Graphing Calculator.

Solved examples

An exponential growth model

Problem. An account starts at $500 and grows 4% per year: f(x) = 500(1.04)^x. How much is in it after 5 years?

  1. Growth of 4% per year means a growth factor of 1.04.
  2. f(5) = 500(1.04)^5 = 500(1.21665) ≈ 608.33.
  3. To graph it, plot 5(1.04)^x so the amounts are in hundreds of dollars and fit the window.

Answer. About $608.33 after 5 years. Graph it in the calculator

A linear-quadratic system

Problem. Find where y = 2x − 3 and y = x² − 4x + 3 intersect.

  1. Set them equal: x² − 4x + 3 = 2x − 3, so x² − 6x + 6 = 0.
  2. Quadratic formula: x = (6 ± √(36 − 24))/2 = 3 ± √3.
  3. So x ≈ 4.732 or x ≈ 1.268, and y = 2x − 3 gives y ≈ 6.464 or y ≈ −0.464.

Answer. The intersections are about (4.732, 6.464) and (1.268, −0.464). Because the NC Math 1 test has a calculator-inactive section, practice solving this by hand as well. Graph it in the calculator

How to use the North Carolina (NC) Graphing Calculator

  • Type an equation. Write it in terms of x, for example x^2 - 4 or 2x + 1. Circles, ellipses and inequalities work too: try x^2 + y^2 = 9 or y > 2x + 1 (shaded). On a phone, tap the equation box and use the built-in math keyboard for powers, roots, fractions and trig.
  • Compare curves. Tap + to add more equations. Intersections, roots, intercepts and turning points are marked automatically, and you can tap any entry under Key points to jump to it.
  • Use sliders. Any extra letter becomes a slider, so a*x^2 + b*x + c lets you drag a, b and c.
  • Move around. Drag to pan, pinch or scroll to zoom, or use the + / − buttons. The home button resets the view and the corner button opens a full-screen graph.
  • Plot points and fit a regression. Type a point such as (2, 5) or a list like (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 5). For real data, open Data table & regression, paste x, y pairs and pick linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential, power or logarithmic: the best-fit equation, R² and a prediction box appear instantly.
  • Degrees or radians. Tap RAD / DEG beside the equations to switch angle mode.
  • Name, share or save. Edit the graph title above the plot. Settings lets you download a PNG or JPG, or open a print-ready page to save as PDF. The share button copies a link that reopens your equations and graph title.
  • Practice for tests. Turn on US Exam Mode in Settings to lock key-point hints and tooltips, like a real testing calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the official calculator for North Carolina tests?

No. ExamGrapher is an independent practice tool and is not affiliated with the North Carolina Department of Public Instruction. Check your school or the state's guidance for the calculators allowed on EOG and EOC tests.

What can I practice with it?

Common school algebra graphs: linear equations, quadratics, exponentials, systems of equations and inequalities. Enter an equation and the roots, intercepts and intersections are marked for you.

Can I practice without the hints?

Yes. Turn on US Exam Mode in Settings to lock key-point hints and tooltips.

Will it work on a school Chromebook or phone?

It runs in a normal web browser with no install. Some schools block certain websites, so it may depend on your network.

Which North Carolina math tests could I practice for here?

The graphing skills apply to EOG and EOC math, including NC Math 1 and NC Math 3, which are the high-school math EOC tests. Which tests allow a calculator, and which kind, can change, so check the current NCDPI guidance or ask your teacher.
