Z-Score Calculator

About this calculator

Updated November 2026 · Free, runs in your browser

A z-score tells you how many standard deviations a value sits from the mean. Positive means above the mean, negative means below, and zero means exactly average. This calculator converts a raw value to a z-score, turns a z-score or percentile back into a value, and finds the probability (area under the normal curve) for one tail, a middle range or both tails.

It assumes the data is roughly normal, which is the usual setting for z-score and percentile questions in AP Statistics and introductory college statistics.

Closely related topics are covered by Normal Distribution & Bell Curve Grapher, Bell Curve Generator, Standard Deviation Calculator and Confidence Interval Calculator.

Disclaimer: ExamGrapher's statistics tools are for learning and for checking homework. Everything is calculated in your browser, and nothing you type is uploaded. For research, medical, financial or other high-stakes decisions, confirm results with dedicated statistical software.

The z-score formula

z = (x − μ) / σ, where x is the value, μ is the mean and σ is the standard deviation. To go back from a z-score to a value, use x = μ + zσ. If you only have a sample, the same formula works with the sample mean and sample standard deviation.

Worked example

A test has a mean of 70 and a standard deviation of 10. A score of 85 gives z = (85 − 70) / 10 = 1.5. The area to the left of z = 1.5 is about 0.9332, so the score is at about the 93rd percentile and roughly 6.7% of scores are higher. The calculator opens with these numbers already filled in.

What the probability options mean

  • Left tail, P(X < a): the share of values below a. This is the percentile.
  • Right tail, P(X > a): the share of values above a.
  • Between a and b: the probability that a value falls inside the range.
  • Outside a and b: the probability in both tails together, which is what two-sided tests use.

The 68-95-99.7 rule

In a normal distribution about 68% of values fall within 1 standard deviation of the mean (z between −1 and 1), about 95% within 2, and about 99.7% within 3. That is why values beyond z = ±2 are called unusual and beyond ±3 are rare.

Z-scores worth remembering

  • z = 1.645 is the 95th percentile (a 5% right tail).
  • z = 1.96 is the 97.5th percentile and marks the middle 95%.
  • z = 2.576 is the 99.5th percentile and marks the middle 99%.

Practice problems with answers

  • Scores have mean 100 and standard deviation 15. A score of 130 gives z = (130 - 100)/15 = 2. The area to the left is about 0.9772, so it is near the 98th percentile.
  • What share of scores fall between 85 and 115? Those are z = -1 and z = 1, which hold about 0.6827 (68.27%) of a normal distribution.
  • A test has mean 70 and standard deviation 10. What score marks the 90th percentile? The 90th percentile is z of about 1.2816, so x = 70 + 1.2816(10), about 82.8.

Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong standard deviation. Use the population value if you know it, and the sample value otherwise.
  • Dropping the sign. A negative z-score means the value is below the mean, so the left tail is smaller than 0.5.
  • Reading a z-score as a probability. The z-score is a distance; the probability is the area under the curve at that distance.
  • Using normal-curve areas on strongly skewed data. A z-score is still computable, but the percentile from the normal table will not be accurate.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good z-score?

It depends on what you are measuring. A z-score only says how far a value is from the mean. For test scores a high positive z means a high percentile, while for something like error rates a low or negative z is better. In a normal distribution, values beyond ±2 are unusual and beyond ±3 are rare.

Can a z-score be negative?

Yes. A negative z-score means the value is below the mean. A z-score of −1.2 is 1.2 standard deviations below average.

How do I find a z-score from a percentile?

Choose “Z-score and value from a percentile”, then enter the percentile. For example, the 90th percentile is z ≈ 1.2816.

What is the difference between a z-score and a t-score?

A z-score uses the normal distribution, normally with a known or assumed standard deviation. A t statistic uses the sample standard deviation and the t distribution, which has heavier tails when the sample is small. For tests on means from small samples, use the t-test calculator.

Does it work if my data is not normal?

You can always compute a z-score, but the probabilities and percentiles assume a normal distribution. For skewed data they can be misleading, so check a histogram first.

Is a z-score the same as a standard score?

Yes. Standard score and z-score mean the same thing: the number of standard deviations a value is from the mean.
