About this tool

Graph a system of inequalities and see the overlapping solution region shaded. A free online tool for algebra, linear programming and SAT prep.

A system of inequalities asks for the points that satisfy several conditions at once. Each inequality shades one region, and the answer is the overlap, often called the feasible region. This is the graphical heart of linear programming, where limits on time, money or materials combine into a shape and the best answer sits at a corner of that shape.

Enter each inequality in its own row and look for the area where all the shading agrees. Boundaries drawn dashed are excluded, and solid ones are included. The corner points of the overlap come from where two boundary lines intersect, so solve those pairs of equations to find exact coordinates. With the default pair, y > x + 2 and y < -2x + 5, the wedge-shaped region has its tip at (1, 3).

  • The solution set is only the overlap of every shaded region.
  • Corner points are the intersections of boundary lines, found by solving two equations at a time.
  • Solid and dashed edges still matter: a dashed corner point is not part of the solution.
  • An empty overlap means the system has no solution, as when two regions face away from each other.

Solved examples

Overlap of two half-planes

Problem. Graph y > x + 2 and y < -2x + 5. Is (0, 3) a solution?

  1. The boundaries meet where x + 2 = -2x + 5, so x = 1 and y = 3: the corner is (1, 3).
  2. Test (0, 3): 3 > 0 + 2 is true, and 3 < 0 + 5 is true.
  3. The solution is the wedge above the first line and below the second.

Answer. Yes, (0, 3) is in the solution region; the boundaries meet at (1, 3). Graph it in the calculator

A triangular region

Problem. Graph y >= 0, y <= 4 - x and y <= 2x. Find the region's vertices and area.

  1. y = 0 meets y = 4 - x at (4, 0). y = 0 meets y = 2x at (0, 0).
  2. 2x = 4 - x gives x = 4/3 and y = 8/3.
  3. Area = (1/2)(base 4)(height 8/3) = 16/3.

Answer. Vertices (0, 0), (4, 0), (4/3, 8/3); area 16/3, about 5.33. Graph it in the calculator

How to use the System of Inequalities Grapher

  • Rewrite each inequality. Solve for y so they look like y > x + 2 and y < -2x + 5.
  • Enter them one by one. Each gets its own shaded color. Their overlap is visible as a darker area.
  • Identify the overlap. Zoom until the whole overlapping region is visible, including any corners.
  • Find the corners. Turn the two relevant boundaries into equations and solve them, or read the intersection from a plotted pair of lines.
  • Check a point inside. Pick one point in the overlap and test it in every original inequality.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the solution region of a system?

Graph every inequality and find where all the shaded areas overlap. Any point inside that overlap satisfies every inequality.

How do I get the corner points?

Replace the inequality signs with equals signs for the two boundaries that meet at the corner, then solve that pair of equations.

Can a system of inequalities have no solution?

Yes. If the shaded regions never overlap, such as y > x + 3 and y < x - 1, no point satisfies both.

What if the region is unbounded?

Some overlaps extend forever in one direction. They have corner points but no outer edge, so a maximum may not exist even if a minimum does.

How is this used in linear programming?

Constraints form the region, and the objective, such as profit, is tested at each corner. The best value always occurs at a corner point.
