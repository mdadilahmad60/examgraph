About this tool

Updated November 2026 · Free, no sign-up

Free AP Calculus AB and BC practice calculator. Graph functions, compare a curve with its derivative, find roots and check free-response work.

AP Calculus AB and BC include sections where a graphing calculator is expected, and the College Board lists the calculator capabilities it assumes students can use, such as graphing a function in a viewing window and finding zeros or intersections. The rules for allowed models and for what the exam requires can change, so read the current course and exam description and the College Board calculator policy before test day.

In 2026 the built-in Desmos graphing calculator in Bluebook is available only in the calculator-required parts of the exam, and only the version inside Bluebook may be used, not the website or app. This page is for practice, not for the exam itself. It graphs functions, draws a derivative with d/dx( ) and shows the exact formula and rules in the Formula & steps panel, shades a definite integral with int(f, a, b), draws slope fields with dy/dx = f(x, y), and marks roots, extrema and intersections. Use these to check your own work, not to replace the justification that free-response questions ask for. The rules for what a calculator may do on the exam can change, so check the current College Board policy. The default pair is f(x) = x^3 - 3x and its derivative, whose roots line up with the peaks and valleys of f.

  • Graph f and d/dx(f) together to see where f increases, decreases and turns.
  • Use int(f, a, b) to shade and check an area or accumulation answer, with an antiderivative and F(b) - F(a) for standard integrals.
  • Use dy/dx = f(x, y), y(0) = 1 to draw a slope field and a solution curve for BC differential equations.
  • Zoom in on a point to see the curve become a line, the idea behind differentiability.
  • Sliders help you explore how a parameter changes a limit, a slope or a critical point.

Closely related topics are covered by AP Precalculus Graphing Calculator, Digital SAT Math Graphing Calculator and ACT Math Practice Graphing Calculator.

Solved examples

Find a maximum, then confirm it with the derivative

Problem. Find the maximum value of f(x) = x²e^(−x) for x ≥ 0.

  1. Use the product rule: f′(x) = 2x·e^(−x) − x²·e^(−x) = x(2 − x)e^(−x).
  2. Set f′(x) = 0: x = 0 or x = 2. Since e^(−x) is always positive, the sign of f′ is the sign of x(2 − x).
  3. f′ is positive for 0 < x < 2 and negative for x > 2, so f has a maximum at x = 2.
  4. f(2) = 4e^(−2) ≈ 0.541.

Answer. The maximum is 4e^(−2) ≈ 0.541 at x = 2. The Key points list shows the same point. On the exam, show the sign analysis rather than citing the graph. Graph it in the calculator

A definite integral with a clean antiderivative

Problem. Evaluate the integral of x²e^(−x) from 0 to 2.

  1. An antiderivative is F(x) = −e^(−x)(x² + 2x + 2); differentiate it to confirm that F′(x) = x²e^(−x).
  2. F(2) = −e^(−2)(4 + 4 + 2) = −10e^(−2) ≈ −1.353.
  3. F(0) = −(2) = −2.
  4. F(2) − F(0) = 2 − 10e^(−2) ≈ 0.647.

Answer. The area is 2 − 10e^(−2) ≈ 0.647. Shade it with int(x^2*exp(-x), 0, 2) to check the value. Graph it in the calculator

How to use the AP Calculus Online Graphing Calculator

  • Type an equation. Write it in terms of x, for example x^2 - 4 or 2x + 1. Circles, ellipses and inequalities work too: try x^2 + y^2 = 9 or y > 2x + 1 (shaded). On a phone, tap the equation box and use the built-in math keyboard for powers, roots, fractions and trig.
  • Compare curves. Tap + to add more equations. Intersections, roots, intercepts and turning points are marked automatically, and you can tap any entry under Key points to jump to it.
  • Use sliders. Any extra letter becomes a slider, so a*x^2 + b*x + c lets you drag a, b and c.
  • Move around. Drag to pan, pinch or scroll to zoom, or use the + / − buttons. The home button resets the view and the corner button opens a full-screen graph.
  • Plot points and fit a regression. Type a point such as (2, 5) or a list like (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 5). For real data, open Data table & regression, paste x, y pairs and pick linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential, power or logarithmic: the best-fit equation, R² and a prediction box appear instantly.
  • Degrees or radians. Tap RAD / DEG beside the equations to switch angle mode.
  • Name, share or save. Edit the graph title above the plot. Settings lets you download a PNG or JPG, or open a print-ready page to save as PDF. The share button copies a link that reopens your equations and graph title.
  • Practice for tests. Turn on US Exam Mode in Settings to lock key-point hints and tooltips, like a real testing calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use ExamGrapher on the AP Calculus exam?

No. It is an independent practice tool, not an approved exam device. AP exams have their own calculator rules, and some parts do not allow a calculator at all, so read the College Board's current policy.

Can it draw derivatives, integrals and slope fields?

Yes. d/dx(f) draws a derivative and shows its exact formula and steps, int(f, a, b) shades an area and shows the value (with F(b) - F(a) for standard integrals), and dy/dx = f(x, y) draws a slope field. Do the calculus by hand and use these to check, and read the College Board's current policy for what is allowed on the exam.

How can I explore how a parameter changes a graph?

Use any extra letter as a slider. Typing a*x^2 + b*x + c lets you drag a, b and c and watch the curve change.

Is it free to use?

Yes. It is free, needs no sign-in, and works in any modern browser.

What is local linearity?

Zoom in far enough on a differentiable function and it looks like a straight line. That line is the tangent, and its slope is the derivative. A corner, like |x| at 0, stays a corner at every zoom level.
