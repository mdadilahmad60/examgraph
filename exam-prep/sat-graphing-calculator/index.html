About this tool

Updated November 2026 · Free, no sign-up

Practice the Digital SAT Math section with a free graphing calculator. Solve systems by intersection, plot parabolas and check answers fast.

Since the US switch in March 2024, the SAT has been fully digital and adaptive, and a Desmos graphing calculator is built into the Bluebook app for the entire Math section (you can switch it to a scientific calculator, or bring an approved handheld one). Students who can graph a system, a quadratic or a data set quickly gain real time on every module.

Practice the high-value moves here: intersections for systems, vertex and roots for quadratics, shaded inequalities, and regressions for table and scatter-plot questions in the Data table & regression panel.

The SAT's testing app has included a graphing calculator for the whole Math section, but policies and app features can change, so check the College Board's current guidance before test day. Because the section is adaptive, saving time on routine items such as intersections and vertices leaves more minutes for the harder questions in the second module.

  • Graph a system of equations and read the intersection instead of solving it by hand.
  • Read the vertex and roots of a quadratic from the graph, then check with algebra if there is time.
  • Use sliders for a parameter question, such as finding the value of k that gives one solution.
  • For data questions, paste the table into Data table & regression to see the fitted line.

Solved examples

A system from a word problem

Problem. One gym charges a $30 joining fee plus $20 per month. Another has no joining fee and charges $26 per month. After how many months is the total cost the same?

  1. Write each cost as a function of months x: y = 20x + 30 and y = 26x.
  2. Set them equal: 20x + 30 = 26x, so 30 = 6x and x = 5.
  3. Substitute back: y = 26(5) = 130. Check the other gym: 20(5) + 30 = 130.
  4. On the graph the two lines cross at (5, 130).

Answer. After 5 months both gyms cost $130 in total. Graph it in the calculator

A parameter that gives exactly one solution

Problem. For which value of k does kx² + 4x + 1 = 0 have exactly one real solution (k is not 0)?

  1. A quadratic has exactly one real solution when its discriminant is zero: b² − 4ac = 0.
  2. Here a = k, b = 4, c = 1, so 16 − 4k = 0.
  3. Solve: k = 4.
  4. Check: 4x² + 4x + 1 = (2x + 1)², which touches the x-axis once, at x = −1/2.

Answer. k = 4. Drag k to 4 on the graph and the parabola just touches the x-axis. Graph it in the calculator

How to use the Digital SAT Math Graphing Calculator

  • Type an equation. Write it in terms of x, for example x^2 - 4 or 2x + 1. Circles, ellipses and inequalities work too: try x^2 + y^2 = 9 or y > 2x + 1 (shaded). On a phone, tap the equation box and use the built-in math keyboard for powers, roots, fractions and trig.
  • Compare curves. Tap + to add more equations. Intersections, roots, intercepts and turning points are marked automatically, and you can tap any entry under Key points to jump to it.
  • Use sliders. Any extra letter becomes a slider, so a*x^2 + b*x + c lets you drag a, b and c.
  • Move around. Drag to pan, pinch or scroll to zoom, or use the + / − buttons. The home button resets the view and the corner button opens a full-screen graph.
  • Plot points and fit a regression. Type a point such as (2, 5) or a list like (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 5). For real data, open Data table & regression, paste x, y pairs and pick linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential, power or logarithmic: the best-fit equation, R² and a prediction box appear instantly.
  • Degrees or radians. Tap RAD / DEG beside the equations to switch angle mode.
  • Name, share or save. Edit the graph title above the plot. Settings lets you download a PNG or JPG, or open a print-ready page to save as PDF. The share button copies a link that reopens your equations and graph title.
  • Practice for tests. Turn on US Exam Mode in Settings to lock key-point hints and tooltips, like a real testing calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Is ExamGrapher the calculator used on the Digital SAT?

No. ExamGrapher is an independent practice tool and is not affiliated with the College Board. Check the College Board's official calculator policy for what you can use on test day, and use this page to practice graphing skills.

How do I practice SAT-style graphing questions here?

Type each equation into its own row, for example 3x + 2 and -x + 6. Where the two lines meet is marked automatically and listed under Key points, so you can check an intersection you worked out by hand.

What does US Exam Mode do?

It turns off key-point hints and tooltips so you graph without help, which is closer to working with a basic test calculator. Switch it on in Settings.

Do I need an account or an app?

No. It is free, needs no sign-in, and runs in your phone or computer browser.

Which kinds of problems benefit most from graphing?

Systems of equations, quadratics with a vertex or root question, inequalities and any problem with a parameter. Practice them under US Exam Mode so you do not depend on the hints.
