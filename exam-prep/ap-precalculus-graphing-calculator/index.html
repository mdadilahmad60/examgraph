About this tool

Updated November 2026 · Free, no sign-up

Free AP Precalculus practice graphing calculator. Plot polynomial, exponential, log and trig functions and explore regressions online.

AP Precalculus is the newest AP math course: it launched in fall 2023 and the first exam was given in May 2024. It leans heavily on graphing-calculator skills: the exam requires a graphing calculator on Part B of the multiple-choice section and Part A of the free-response section, and a built-in Desmos graphing calculator is available through Bluebook.

The course is built around modeling. You are expected to fit linear, quadratic, exponential, logarithmic and sinusoidal models to data, interpret the parameters, and compare rates of change. This page opens with a sinusoidal model you can tune with sliders, and the Data table & regression panel handles the fitting questions.

  • Unit 1: polynomial and rational functions, including end behavior and asymptotes.
  • Unit 2: exponential and logarithmic functions and models.
  • Unit 3: trigonometric and polar functions.
  • Unit 4 (classroom only, not on the exam): parametric, vector and matrix functions.

You may also find these tools useful: Digital SAT Math Graphing Calculator, Digital PSAT Graphing Calculator, ACT Math Practice Graphing Calculator and AP Calculus Online Graphing Calculator.

Solved examples

Is this table exponential? Build the model

Problem. A table gives y = 50, 40, 32, 25.6 for x = 0, 1, 2, 3. Find a model and predict y at x = 5.

  1. Check for a constant ratio: 40/50 = 0.8, 32/40 = 0.8 and 25.6/32 = 0.8.
  2. A constant ratio means exponential decay with initial value 50 and growth factor 0.8: y = 50(0.8)^x.
  3. The factor 0.8 means a 20% decrease per unit of x.
  4. Predict: y(5) = 50(0.8)^5 = 50(0.32768) = 16.384.

Answer. y = 50(0.8)^x, and y(5) ≈ 16.38. Paste the table into Data table & regression and choose Exponential to confirm. Graph it in the calculator

Write a sinusoidal model from its features

Problem. A tide height has a maximum of 7 ft, a minimum of 1 ft, one full cycle every 12 hours, and starts at its midline going up. Write a model.

  1. Amplitude = (7 − 1)/2 = 3 and midline = (7 + 1)/2 = 4.
  2. Period 12 means b = 2π/12 = π/6.
  3. Starting at the midline going up matches a sine with no shift: h(t) = 3 sin(πt/6) + 4.
  4. Check: h(3) = 3(1) + 4 = 7 (maximum) and h(9) = 3(−1) + 4 = 1 (minimum).

Answer. h(t) = 3 sin(πt/6) + 4. Graph it in the calculator

How to use the AP Precalculus Graphing Calculator

  • Type an equation. Write it in terms of x, for example x^2 - 4 or 2x + 1. Circles, ellipses and inequalities work too: try x^2 + y^2 = 9 or y > 2x + 1 (shaded). On a phone, tap the equation box and use the built-in math keyboard for powers, roots, fractions and trig.
  • Compare curves. Tap + to add more equations. Intersections, roots, intercepts and turning points are marked automatically, and you can tap any entry under Key points to jump to it.
  • Use sliders. Any extra letter becomes a slider, so a*x^2 + b*x + c lets you drag a, b and c.
  • Move around. Drag to pan, pinch or scroll to zoom, or use the + / − buttons. The home button resets the view and the corner button opens a full-screen graph.
  • Plot points and fit a regression. Type a point such as (2, 5) or a list like (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 5). For real data, open Data table & regression, paste x, y pairs and pick linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential, power or logarithmic: the best-fit equation, R² and a prediction box appear instantly.
  • Degrees or radians. Tap RAD / DEG beside the equations to switch angle mode.
  • Name, share or save. Edit the graph title above the plot. Settings lets you download a PNG or JPG, or open a print-ready page to save as PDF. The share button copies a link that reopens your equations and graph title.
  • Practice for tests. Turn on US Exam Mode in Settings to lock key-point hints and tooltips, like a real testing calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Is a graphing calculator allowed on the AP Precalculus exam?

Yes, on the calculator-active parts of the exam. Part of the multiple-choice section and part of the free-response section require a graphing calculator, and the rest is calculator-free. Check the College Board's current AP calculator policy for the approved list.

Which calculator skills does AP Precalculus test?

Graphing a function, finding zeros and intersections, computing a regression (linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential, logarithmic or sinusoidal-style models) and evaluating a model at a given input.

Can I run a regression here?

Yes. Open Data table & regression under the equations, paste x, y pairs and choose a model. You get the equation, R² and a prediction box, and the points and curve are graphed together.

Is this an official AP tool?

No. ExamGrapher is an independent free practice tool and is not affiliated with the College Board.
