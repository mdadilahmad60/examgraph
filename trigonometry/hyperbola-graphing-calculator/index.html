About this tool

Graph a hyperbola from its equation and see both branches and the asymptotes. A free online grapher for precalculus and conics review.

A hyperbola has two separate branches that open away from each other, each bending toward a pair of crossing lines called asymptotes. In x²/a² - y²/b² = 1 the branches open left and right, with vertices at (±a, 0). If the minus sign comes before x² instead, the branches open up and down. The minus sign between the squared terms is the giveaway that you are looking at a hyperbola.

The asymptotes of x²/4 - y²/9 = 1 are y = ±(3/2)x, found by replacing the 1 with a 0 and solving. The foci lie on the same axis as the vertices, at distance c from the center where c² = a² + b², which differs from the ellipse rule by a sign. Here c = √13, about 3.6. The curve never touches its asymptotes, but gets closer the farther out you go.

  • The positive squared term tells you which way the branches open.
  • Asymptotes come from setting the right side to zero: y = ±(b/a)x for the horizontal case.
  • The foci satisfy c² = a² + b², further from the center than the vertices.
  • A rectangular hyperbola has a = b, so its asymptotes are perpendicular.

Closely related topics are covered by Conic Sections Grapher, Circle Equation Grapher and Ellipse Equation Grapher.

Solved examples

Asymptotes and foci

Problem. Analyze x^2/4 - y^2/9 = 1.

  1. The x^2 term is positive, so the branches open left and right with a = 2 and b = 3. Vertices (+-2, 0).
  2. Asymptotes: y = +-(b/a)x = +-1.5x.
  3. c^2 = a^2 + b^2 = 13, so the foci are (+-sqrt(13), 0), about (+-3.606, 0).

Answer. Vertices (+-2, 0), asymptotes y = +-1.5x, foci about (+-3.606, 0). Graph it in the calculator

A vertical hyperbola

Problem. Analyze y^2/9 - x^2/16 = 1.

  1. The y^2 term is positive, so the branches open up and down: a = 3, b = 4. Vertices (0, +-3).
  2. Asymptotes: y = +-(a/b)x = +-0.75x.
  3. c^2 = 9 + 16 = 25, so the foci are (0, +-5).

Answer. Vertices (0, +-3), asymptotes y = +-0.75x, foci (0, +-5). Graph it in the calculator

How to use the Hyperbola Equation Grapher

  • Enter the equation. Type x^2/4 - y^2/9 = 1 and look at the two branches.
  • Find the vertices. Read where each branch crosses the axis and compare with ±a.
  • Draw the asymptotes. Add y = 1.5x and y = -1.5x as lines and see how the branches follow them.
  • Swap the signs. Put the minus sign in front of x² instead to rotate the branches to up and down.
  • Shift the center. Replace x and y with (x - 1) and (y + 2) to move the hyperbola to a new center.

Frequently asked questions

How do I tell which way a hyperbola opens?

Look for the positive squared term. If x² is positive the branches open left and right; if y² is positive they open up and down.

How do I find the asymptotes?

Set the right-hand side to zero and solve for y. For x²/4 - y²/9 = 0, y = ±(3/2)x.

How do I find the foci?

Use c² = a² + b². For a = 2 and b = 3, c = √13 ≈ 3.61, so the foci are at (±3.61, 0).

How is a hyperbola different from an ellipse?

An ellipse has a plus sign and is a closed loop. A hyperbola has a minus sign and has two open branches.

What is a rectangular hyperbola?

One with a = b. Its asymptotes meet at right angles. The graph of xy = 1 is the same curve, rotated 45 degrees.
