Confidence Interval Calculator

About this calculator

Updated November 2026 · Free, runs in your browser

A confidence interval gives a range of plausible values for a population parameter based on a sample. This calculator builds an interval for a population mean from a sample mean, standard deviation and sample size, or for a proportion from a count of successes. Choose 90%, 95%, 99% or your own confidence level.

To keep practicing, try these related tools: Z-Score Calculator, Normal Distribution & Bell Curve Grapher, Bell Curve Generator, Standard Deviation Calculator and T-Test Calculator.

Disclaimer: ExamGrapher's statistics tools are for learning and for checking homework. Everything is calculated in your browser, and nothing you type is uploaded. For research, medical, financial or other high-stakes decisions, confirm results with dedicated statistical software.

Formulas

  • Mean, σ unknown: x̄ ± t* · s/√n, with n − 1 degrees of freedom
  • Mean, σ known: x̄ ± z* · σ/√n
  • Proportion, Wald: p̂ ± z*√(p̂(1 − p̂)/n)
  • Proportion, Wilson: re-centres and adjusts the width, and works better for small samples or proportions near 0 or 1.

Worked example

A sample of n = 36 has a mean of 52.4 and a standard deviation of 6.8. The standard error is 6.8 / 6 = 1.133. For 95% confidence with 35 degrees of freedom, t* is about 2.030, so the margin of error is about 2.30 and the interval is about (50.10, 54.70). The calculator opens with these numbers.

t or z?

Use t when the population standard deviation is unknown, which is almost always the case with real data. Use z only when the population standard deviation is known from outside your sample. With large samples the two give nearly the same answer.

How to read a confidence interval

A 95% confidence interval does not mean there is a 95% chance the true value lies inside this one interval. It means that if you repeated the sampling many times and built an interval each time, about 95% of those intervals would contain the true value.

Critical values worth remembering

  • 90% confidence: z* = 1.645
  • 95% confidence: z* = 1.960
  • 99% confidence: z* = 2.576

Practice problems with answers

  • A sample of n = 36 has mean 50 and s = 12. The standard error is 12/6 = 2. With 35 degrees of freedom the 95% critical value is about 2.030, so the margin of error is about 4.06 and the interval is about 45.94 to 54.06.
  • In a poll, 120 of 200 people say yes, so the sample proportion is 0.6. The standard error is sqrt(0.6 x 0.4/200), about 0.0346, and the 95% margin of error is 1.96 x 0.0346, about 0.068. The interval is about 0.532 to 0.668.
  • How many people give a 95% margin of error of 0.03 when you have no estimate of p? Use p = 0.5: n = (1.96/0.03)^2 x 0.25, about 1067.1, so round up to 1068.

Common mistakes

  • Saying there is a 95% probability that the true mean is in this particular interval. The 95% describes the method: about 95% of intervals built this way capture the true value.
  • Using z instead of t with a small sample and an estimated standard deviation.
  • Forgetting to round the required sample size up.

Frequently asked questions

What is the margin of error?

It is the critical value times the standard error, which is the distance from the sample estimate to each end of the interval.

How can I get a narrower interval?

Use a larger sample or a lower confidence level. Quadrupling the sample size roughly halves the margin of error.

Is a 99% interval better than a 95% interval?

It is more likely to contain the true value, but it is wider and so less precise. The right level depends on how costly a miss would be.

Which proportion interval should I use?

Many textbooks teach the Wald (normal approximation) interval, so it is shown for checking homework. The Wilson interval is generally more reliable, especially when n is small or the proportion is near 0 or 1.

What conditions do I need?

The sample should be random and the observations independent. For a mean, the data should be roughly normal or the sample reasonably large. For a proportion, a common rule of thumb is at least 10 successes and 10 failures.

What if I only have raw data?
