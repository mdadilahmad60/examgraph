About this tool

Updated November 2026 · Free, no sign-up

Free online graphing calculator for DeltaMath homework. Plot transformations, intersections and AP Classroom problems fast, with no sign-up.

DeltaMath is an online platform that many teachers use for practice and homework in middle-school and high-school math. Problems are often generated with different numbers for each student, and teachers decide which tools students may use on each assignment. Some problems expect a calculator and many do not, so follow your teacher's instructions and check the current assignment settings before you use any outside tool.

ExamGrapher is a separate, independent website, so open it in another tab next to your assignment. It is handy for checking an answer you already worked out: graph the function, read the roots, vertex or intercepts, and see whether they match what you found. When your own result and the graph disagree, you have found the step to recheck, which is usually more useful than just getting the final answer.

  • Use the graph to check your work after you solve a problem by hand.
  • Roots, intercepts and intersections are marked and listed under Key points.
  • Sliders let you see how changing a coefficient changes the graph.
  • Everything runs in the browser with no account, so it works on a school device.

Solved examples

Check a quadratic you solved by hand

Problem. You found that f(x) = x² − 6x + 5 has roots 1 and 5 and a vertex at (3, −4). Is that right?

  1. Check the roots by substitution: f(1) = 1 − 6 + 5 = 0 and f(5) = 25 − 30 + 5 = 0.
  2. Find the vertex: x = −b/(2a) = 6/2 = 3, and f(3) = 9 − 18 + 5 = −4.
  3. Graph the function and compare: the curve crosses the x-axis at 1 and 5 and has its lowest point at (3, −4).

Answer. Yes. If the graph had disagreed, the step to recheck would be your factoring or your vertex formula. Graph it in the calculator

An absolute value equation

Problem. Solve |2x − 3| = 5.

  1. Split into two cases: 2x − 3 = 5 or 2x − 3 = −5.
  2. First case: 2x = 8, so x = 4. Second case: 2x = −2, so x = −1.
  3. Check: |2(4) − 3| = 5 and |2(−1) − 3| = |−5| = 5.
  4. Graph y = |2x − 3| and y = 5; the intersections have x-values 4 and −1.

Answer. x = 4 or x = −1. Graph it in the calculator

How to use the Online Graphing Calculator for DeltaMath

  • Type an equation. Write it in terms of x, for example x^2 - 4 or 2x + 1. Circles, ellipses and inequalities work too: try x^2 + y^2 = 9 or y > 2x + 1 (shaded). On a phone, tap the equation box and use the built-in math keyboard for powers, roots, fractions and trig.
  • Compare curves. Tap + to add more equations. Intersections, roots, intercepts and turning points are marked automatically, and you can tap any entry under Key points to jump to it.
  • Use sliders. Any extra letter becomes a slider, so a*x^2 + b*x + c lets you drag a, b and c.
  • Move around. Drag to pan, pinch or scroll to zoom, or use the + / − buttons. The home button resets the view and the corner button opens a full-screen graph.
  • Plot points and fit a regression. Type a point such as (2, 5) or a list like (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 5). For real data, open Data table & regression, paste x, y pairs and pick linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential, power or logarithmic: the best-fit equation, R² and a prediction box appear instantly.
  • Degrees or radians. Tap RAD / DEG beside the equations to switch angle mode.
  • Name, share or save. Edit the graph title above the plot. Settings lets you download a PNG or JPG, or open a print-ready page to save as PDF. The share button copies a link that reopens your equations and graph title.
  • Practice for tests. Turn on US Exam Mode in Settings to lock key-point hints and tooltips, like a real testing calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this inside a DeltaMath assignment?

ExamGrapher is a separate website, so open it in another tab or window. Ask your teacher what tools are allowed for each assignment.

Is ExamGrapher connected to DeltaMath?

No. ExamGrapher is an independent tool and is not affiliated with DeltaMath.

How do I graph a function to check my work?

Type it in the equation box, for example 2x + 1 or x^2 - 3x + 2. Use the + and minus buttons or pinch to zoom, and tap a Key point to jump to a root or intercept.

Can I save or share my graph?

Yes. The share button copies a link that reopens your equations, and Settings lets you download the graph as a PNG image.

Is it okay to use this for homework?

That depends on your teacher and the assignment. Some teachers allow a graphing tool for checking work and others do not, so ask before you use it.
